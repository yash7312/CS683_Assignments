\section{Task 3: SPP with Prefetch-Aware Dynamic I-RRIP}

\subsection{What the L2 sees from SPP}
SPP (Task 1) sits at the L1D. Each prefetch first looks up the L1D. If it misses there, it goes to the L2 as a
\code{PREFETCH} request, and if it misses in the L2 too, the line is filled into the L2 on its way to the L1D.
(SPP fills the L1D when fewer than half of the L1D MSHRs are busy; otherwise it fills only the L2.) So the replacement
policy sees three new events:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a prefetch fill,
  \item a prefetch request that hits a line already in the L2, and
  \item later, perhaps, a demand hit on a line that a prefetch brought in (\code{BLOCK::prefetch} is still set; the
        cache clears it right after calling the policy).
\end{itemize}
Counting these events shows that only 2.3\% / 0.8\% / 0.9\% of the prefetched lines filled into the L2 ever get a
demand hit at the L2 (columns \emph{pf fills} and \emph{first use} in Table~\ref{tab:t3-var}, counted over warm-up and
simulation). Useful prefetches are consumed in the L1D. The L2 copy is a duplicate that the program will not ask the L2
for again unless the L1D loses it first.

\subsection{Prefetch-aware design}
The prefetch handling is part of \code{replacement/dyn\_irrip.l2c\_repl} (\code{DYN\_PF\_AWARE}, on by default). It
applies to data prefetches in all sets and in both follower modes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Insertion:} a prefetch fill is inserted at RRPV 3 (distant). A prefetch is a guess, not a demand, and
        it will most likely be used in the L1D. In I-RRIP mode data gets RRPV 3 anyway; in SRRIP mode (Trace1) this
        stops prefetched lines from pushing out demand data inserted at 2.
  \item \textbf{No promotion on a prefetch hit:} a prefetch request that hits in the L2 is about to copy the line into
        the L1D, which says nothing about future L2 reuse. SRRIP and I-RRIP would move the line to RRPV 0.
  \item \textbf{First demand use:} the first demand hit on a prefetched line proves the prefetch right, so the line is
        promoted to RRPV 0 like any reused line.
  \item \textbf{Eviction:} among the RRPV-3 candidates, an unused prefetched data line is evicted first, then other
        data, then instructions.
  \item \textbf{Set dueling sees demand traffic only:} prefetch misses are not counted in PSEL (or in the mode-0 ratio).
        Prefetch traffic depends on the prefetcher, not on how well a replacement policy keeps instruction or data
        lines. Counting it would add data misses to both leader groups and dilute the instruction-miss signal the duel
        relies on.
\end{enumerate}
None of this needs new state: \code{BLOCK::prefetch} already exists in every cache line. With no prefetcher none of
these paths runs, so the Task 2B results are exactly the results of this file.

\textbf{Policy.} With SPP added, the L2 policies keep their Task 2 order: SRRIP $+1.18\%$, I-RRIP $+2.14\%$ and
prefetch-unaware Dyn-I-RRIP $+2.19\%$ (SPP + LRU: $+1.15\%$). The instruction-miss savings of I-RRIP add on top of the
data-miss savings of SPP. Dyn-I-RRIP beats static I-RRIP with SPP as well, again by leaving Trace1 on SRRIP
(Trace1: 1.0080 vs 1.0055).

\textbf{Prefetch rules, one at a time.} The complete prefetch-aware policy ($+2.21\%$) and the prefetch-unaware one
($+2.19\%$) are a tie. Since I-RRIP already inserts every data line at RRPV 3, prefetched or not, most prefetch rules
repeat what the unaware policy does in I-RRIP mode anyway. Removing single rules from the aware policy shows which
ones carry weight:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Distant insertion matters.} Inserting prefetches at RRPV 2 instead of 3 costs 0.24\%. The prefetched
        lines then outlive other data lines and push out instruction lines: the L2 instruction MPKI rises on Trace2
        (12.59 $\to$ 12.90) and Trace3 (0.75 $\to$ 0.85).
  \item \textbf{Keeping prefetches out of the duel matters.} Counting prefetch misses in PSEL costs 0.19\%. These
        extra misses are data misses that both leader groups suffer alike, so they add noise but no signal. On Trace3,
        which has few L2 demand misses, they drag PSEL towards SRRIP (I-RRIP chosen in 0.43 of the decisions instead of 1.00).
  \item \textbf{First-use promotion and prefetch-first eviction are ties} (promotion to RRPV 1: $+2.21\%$, to RRPV 2:
        $+2.20\%$, no prefetch-first eviction: $+2.21\%$, vs $+2.21\%$). Only 2.3\% / 0.8\% / 0.9\% of the
        prefetched lines are ever used at the L2, so the first-use rule rarely runs. And with data inserted at RRPV 3,
        the RRPV-3 candidates are nearly always prefetched or other dead data lines anyway. We keep both rules because
        they are free and follow the same reasoning, but the data does not show that they help.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Outcome.} SPP + prefetch-aware Dyn-I-RRIP reaches a geometric-mean speedup of $\boldsymbol{+2.21\%}$, against
$+1.15\%$ for SPP alone (with LRU) and $+1.02\%$ for Dyn-I-RRIP without prefetching, so it beats both. Per trace:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Trace1: 1.0079 vs 1.0083 (SPP) and 0.9993 (Dyn-I-RRIP);
  \item Trace2: 1.0406 vs 1.0135 (SPP) and 1.0268 (Dyn-I-RRIP);
  \item Trace3: 1.0180 vs 1.0126 (SPP) and 1.0048 (Dyn-I-RRIP).
\end{itemize}
The two mechanisms remove different misses, so their gains add up. SPP removes L1D data misses (coverage 8.1\%, L1D
load MPKI 9.21 $\to$ 8.55), and Dyn-I-RRIP removes L2 instruction misses (mean 10.58 $\to$ 7.94), which SPP cannot
touch. On Trace1, where the duel keeps SRRIP, the result stays within 0.04\% of SPP + LRU: the L2 policy has almost no
effect on that trace (LRU and SRRIP differ by 0.04\% there as well).

\textbf{SPP accuracy and coverage} stay the same with every L2 policy (accuracy 54.0\% vs 54.1\%, coverage 8.10\% vs
8.15\% for SPP + LRU vs the submitted combination). The L2 policy does not change what SPP predicts, only how long the
L2 keeps the lines.
